What Usually Goes Wrong in Riverside Homes

Most water damage calls we take in Riverside trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. We see many of these in Riverside homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. The crew from Apple Valley Restoration can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in Riverside began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. A musty smell, peeling paint or dark spotting near a previous repair is worth having checked.

Simple Ways to Keep a Riverside Home Dry

Most of the water damage we see in Riverside homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Start with supply lines. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early.
